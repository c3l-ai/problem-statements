## What is this workshop?

Every publication from the International Conference on Learning Analytics
& Knowledge (LAK) and the Journal of Learning Analytics (JLA) since their
inception, assembled in one open dataset: primary sources, metadata,
incoming and outgoing citations, and problem statements manually extracted
from each paper and coded by question type.

We have already used the dataset for one analysis, examining the kinds of
questions the field has asked over time. We will present it at the
workshop as an example of what the dataset affords.

The rest of the day belongs to your questions. You will form small groups,
scope a question the dataset can support, and start working on it before
the day is out.

## The dataset

**What is in it.** All LAK and JLA publications: primary sources, metadata,
incoming and outgoing citations, and problem statements manually extracted
and coded by question type, following Dillon's classification of research
questions (1984).

**Who maintains it.** The Centre for Change and Complexity in Learning at
Adelaide University, with a yearly update commitment.

**Access.** Access to the dataset is provided during the workshop.

## What we found

Our exemplar analysis asked what kinds of questions the field has asked
across the history of LAK and JLA. The result: research remains
concentrated on describing and relating educational phenomena, with
comparatively limited attention to explanation and intervention. We argue
this reflects a socio-technical misalignment between analytical innovation
and the institutional structures required to sustain it, not a failure of
the research community.

The dataset answered our question. Now it can answer yours.

## Who should come

You, if you hold questions about how learning analytics has developed that
you have not had the data to pursue. In particular:

- Learning analytics researchers conducting meta-research or field-level
  analysis.
- Early-career researchers and PhD candidates seeking systematic
  positioning and gap-finding for their work.

We anticipate 20 to 30 participants, small enough for focused group work
with direct access to the dataset.
